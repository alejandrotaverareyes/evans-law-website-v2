A Fresh Start

Chapter 7

Eliminate debts and move forward

Chapter 7 is the fast bankruptcy chapter that helps people eliminate most kinds of debt at a relatively low cost. Our attorneys are uniquely qualified because we handle every aspect of the case — including the bankruptcy litigation and Chapter 7 exemption planning that some firms overlook. The goal is a case that is low-cost relative to the debt and easy on the client: less stress, and the confidence that your attorney can handle anything that comes up.

Chapter 7 liquidation removes most types of debt from further collection through the discharge, including the power to:

  • Stop lawsuits and other creditor harassment
  • Eliminate most unsecured debt, including credit card and medical bills
  • Stop wage garnishments
  • Assist with tax-related problems
  • Obtain other critical debt relief

When your petition is filed, an automatic stay goes into effect that temporarily halts most collection activity — collection calls, wage garnishments, and ongoing lawsuits. The stay gives you time to sort through reaffirmation, redemption, preference litigation, and judgment-lien stripping. Our attorneys have handled thousands of Chapter 7 cases; if bankruptcy is not right for you, we also provide debt settlement services.

The Super Discharge

Chapter 13

Reorganize, repay, and keep what matters

Chapter 13 has historically been known as the “super discharge.” It offers a higher level of discharge than Chapter 7 over a repayment plan that typically runs 36 to 60 months. After you successfully complete the plan, the court issues a discharge and the fresh start you sought. To be eligible, you need monthly income that can support a minimal plan payment. Unlike Chapter 7, property is not subject to being sold by a Trustee.

A Chapter 13 bankruptcy may enable you to:

  • Gain additional time to repay your debts
  • Discharge all or a portion of your debts
  • Stop foreclosure and strip a second-home mortgage lien
  • Stop a wage garnishment or an auto repossession
  • Address tax-related problems, including discharging tax debt
  • Remove judgment liens and reinstate mortgage debt
  • Reorganize your business or personal affairs

People with significant income or assets who may be ineligible for Chapter 7 can often still qualify for Chapter 13 relief. We help you make an informed decision about which type of bankruptcy — if any — is best for your situation.

Debtor in Possession

Chapter 11

Reorganize and keep operating

Chapter 11 is typically used to reorganize a business — a corporation, sole proprietorship, or partnership. A corporation exists separate from its stockholders, so a corporate Chapter 11 generally does not put shareholders’ personal assets at risk beyond their investment.

A sole proprietorship has no separate identity from its owner, so a case involving one includes both business and personal assets. A partnership exists separately from its partners, but in some partnership cases the partners’ personal assets may be reached, or the partners themselves may be forced to file. We help you choose the structure and strategy that protects what matters most.

A Specialty Area

Taxes & Bankruptcy

Relief from federal and state tax debts

Tax issues are a specialty area for our attorneys, who have assisted people with tax problems over more than 40 years of combined experience. The IRS is a relentless creditor that may garnish wages, seize refunds, levy bank accounts, or take other property — and the California Franchise Tax Board can do much the same. Bankruptcy can provide relief from IRS and state tax debts, either through discharge of the debt and/or a payment plan. A Last Resort, Done Right

Business Closure & Corporate Chapter 7

Business closure and corporate Chapter 7

Chapter 7 of a business involves its liquidation and should be viewed as a last resort, after alternatives — new financing or capital, sale of assets or the business, an out-of-court workout, or a Chapter 11/13 restructuring — have been explored. When a business is insolvent or in the “zone of insolvency,” a developing body of case law holds that management’s duty shifts from owners to creditors.

Filing Chapter 7 ensures creditors’ claims are dealt with in a single unified proceeding and that assets aren’t stripped apart by creditors pursuing individual remedies. Sometimes negotiating settlements outside bankruptcy yields a higher dividend to creditors. Our attorneys have over 35 years of combined experience with business closures and corporate bankruptcy — including the dischargeability trials that can arise — so one practitioner handles both the filing and any litigation that follows.
